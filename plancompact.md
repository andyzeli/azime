AMA is a good skeleton, not a savior, because it was built to answer a different question. The Guides classify by residual state for rating — "disc herniation with radiculopathy, resolved" vs "persistent," single level vs multiple, with or without alteration of motion segment integrity. Those distinctions matter for impairment percentage. Treatment selection turns on different distinctions: is there a radicular component, does imaging correlate with the symptoms, is there a neurological deficit, is this acute or chronic, has this level already been operated. Some overlap, but AMA won't hand you the treatment-relevant splits.

The good news is that when a canonical dx isn't specific enough, the missing piece is almost never another diagnosis. It's a fact about the case. "Microdiscectomy" doesn't apply to disc herniation in general; it applies to disc herniation with radicular symptoms that correlate with the imaging level, after conservative failure. "Fusion" applies to instability, or to post-decompression states. "ESI" applies where there's a radicular component. Those are conditions, not categories.

`adequacy`

is the taxonomy's definition of what a fair trial of that treatment looks like. It's the standard 3A holds the record against when it answers "was this adequately trialed?"

Where it comes from. Same as the rest of the taxonomy: model-drafted, physician-reviewed, versioned. For each treatment, one line describing a sufficient course. PT: "≥8–12 visits over ≥6 weeks with a documented home program and reassessment." ESI: "up to 2–3 injections, spaced ≥2 weeks, with documented response after each." Gabapentin: "titrated to a therapeutic dose for ≥4 weeks." Chiropractic: "≥6 weeks with reassessment." Your examiners will have opinions on every one of these, which is the point — it makes their implicit standard explicit.

What it does in 3A. Without it, the model judges adequacy from general knowledge, which drifts and can't be defended. With it, the model's job becomes a comparison: here's the standard, here's what the record shows (from the episode skeleton plus the text), does the record meet it? The rationale then reads "4 PT visits over 3 weeks, no home program documented; standard is 8–12 over 6 weeks — not adequately trialed" instead of "PT appears to have been limited."

What it does downstream. Adequacy is the hinge for the whole MMI question. A treatment that was tried inadequately is not exhausted runway; it's untried runway that looks tried. That's the single most common way a record misleads toward premature MMI, and the adequacy field is what lets 3A catch it row by row rather than the doctor having to remember the thresholds for every treatment on every case.

**The clinical ladder doesn't change.** Whether the case is workers' comp, auto/PIP, personal injury litigation, or long-term disability, the question "was PT adequately tried for lumbar radiculopathy" has the same clinical answer. What changes is which authority you cite. So keep one ladder and give each entry a `sources` list — ODG section, ACOEM, NASS, AAOS — and let the case type pick which one the rationale leads with. In an Arizona WC case the rationale cites ODG because the ICA adopted it; in a PI case it cites the same entry as evidence-based practice with ODG and the specialty society as support, since nothing is binding there.

**Case type becomes intake metadata**, and it drives three things:

The scope default. "Accepted conditions" is a WC concept. In PI it's the injuries claimed in the demand; in LTD it's the conditions asserted as disabling; in PIP it's what the treatment was billed under. Same slot in intake, different label and source.

The referral questions. WC asks MMI, work capacity, and impairment. PIP asks whether past treatment was reasonable and necessary and related. PI asks causation, permanency, and future medical. LTD asks functional capacity against an occupation. These should be captured at intake as a structured list, not inferred.

What 3B and Pass 4 emphasize. This is the interesting part: the machinery you've built answers more than MMI. "Untried runway" is future medical need in a PI case. "Treatment given outside applicability criteria" is the reasonableness question in PIP. The functional trajectory lens is the core of an LTD opinion. So 3B's and Pass 4's outputs should be parameterized by the referral questions — plateau evidence and adequacy stay as the engine, and the synthesis is framed around what was asked.

**One thing to avoid:** building a separate pipeline or taxonomy per case type. The evidence work is identical; only the framing layer at intake and at synthesis differs. If you keep that boundary clean, adding a case type is adding a referral-question set and a citation preference, not a project.
