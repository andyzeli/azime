#### 17-19b Instructions for Cervical Spine

The following conditions are rated based on radiculopathy: cervical disc herniation, spinal stenosis (foraminal or lateral recess), and degenerative spondylolisthesis. In the case of fractures or Alteration of Motion Segment Integrity (AOMSI) with radiculopathy or myelopathy, WPI impairment rating for radiculopathy is combined with the WPI impairment rating for the fracture or AOMSI.

#### Summary of Possible Impairment Combinations for Cervical Spine Based on the SIEs in Cervical Spine Impairment

**17-19b03**. Cervical AOMSI is only rated in Chapter 17.

**17-19b04**. Cervical myelopathy is only rated in Chapter 13

**17-19b05**. Cervical radiculopathy, when combined with either spinal fractures or AOMSI (but not both), is rated in Chapter 17.

**17-19b06**. Cervical radiculopathy (Chapter 17) may be combined with myelopathy

**17-19b07**. Cervical myelopathyChapter 13 when combined with either a spinal fracture or AOMSI (but not both), is rated in Chapter 17.

**17-19b08**. Cervical radiculopathy, when combined with either spinal fractures or AOMSI (but not both) (Chapter 17), may also be combined with myelopathy Chapter 13
**17-19b09**. Brachial plexus injuries are rated in DBI Table 15-23j Brachial Plexus Impairment upper limb. They may be combined with either spinal fracture or cervical AOMSI (but not both) (Chapter 17), if not duplicative.

**17-19b12**. Cervical radiculopathy, when combined with deep spinal wound infection requiring chronic suppressive antibiotic therapy (6 months or longer) due to chronic osteomyelitis, is rated in Chapter 17.

**17-19b13**. Cervical myelopathy Chapter 13, when combined with deep spinal wound infection requiring chronic suppressive antibiotic therapy (6 months or longer) due to chronic osteomyelitis, is rated in Chapter 17.

**17-19b14**. Cervical radiculopathy, when combined with either spinal fractures or AOMSI (but not both), may also be combined with deep spinal wound infection requiring chronic suppressive antibiotic therapy (6 months or longer) due to chronic osteomyelitis (Chapter 17), and with myelopathy (chapter 13).
