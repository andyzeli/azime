Exam-to-report implementation design

Build three physician screens: Exam, Conclusions (including impairment), and Report review. Keep the existing upstream analyses. Add structured exam facts and physician decisions so drafting can use them directly.

Rating method below: AMA Sixth Edition, original 2008/2009 class-and-grade method. Store the release explicitly; the 2024 spine revision uses a different method. Sources

1. Exam screen: note + required findings

Use the existing visit note as the main input. Beside it, show a short checklist assembled from visitPriority, referral-question gaps, and the facts needed by likely AMA rating rows. The physician can dictate the note or enter checklist findings directly. After dictation, extract proposed answers into the checklist; ask only about missing or conflicting items.

2. MMI and care: one decision per condition

The post-exam pass should answer a practical question: what evidence favors a plateau, and what remaining care might materially change the condition? Show its proposed MMI status, a short rationale, and the relevant treatment rows. The physician accepts or changes the conclusion.

4. AMA impairment: what to build

Build a regional worksheet that asks clinical questions and derives the rating coordinates. The default view should show the selected diagnosis, the facts responsible for its classification, any unanswered criterion, and the resulting percentage. Put numeric modifier controls in an expanded “Rating detail” view.

4.1 Explain the four rating terms in the UI

TermMeaningExample of system behavior
Diagnosis rowThe condition being rated within the regional table.Canonical “disc herniation” retrieves candidate rows. Check each row's entry criteria and footnotes before selecting it.
ClassThe severity category within that diagnosis row. It sets the available impairment range.Use confirmed clinical criteria to select the class. Do not ask the physician to choose an unexplained number.
GradeThe position A–E within the selected class. The original method generally starts at C and adjusts using eligible modifiers.Calculate the movement after functional history, examination and clinical studies are assessed.
Impairment percentageThe value at the selected row + class + grade, in the unit specified by the table.Read the percentage from that cell. A one-grade change is not necessarily one percentage point.

Verify the administered instrument, item wording, anchors, completeness and scoring before mapping it to AMA criteria. Until then, retain it as patient-reported function. The app's regional questionnaires should not be treated as validated named instruments without verification.

4.3 Clinical questions determine the row and class

For a lumbar disc candidate, the worksheet needs the history of objective radiculopathy, its current status, clinical correlation, levels/sides involved, and relevant surgery. Present these as labeled choices with source-backed selections. Then apply the full table criteria. A single question such as “radiculopathy present?” is too coarse.

Why this matters: MRI herniation alone may follow the nonspecific-pain pathway. Prior objective radiculopathy, now resolved, can qualify for the disc-herniation Class 1 pathway. Retrieve the historical findings. [2]

Row selection: preserve all accepted clinical diagnoses, but select the ratable diagnosis according to the regional rules. Do not automatically select the most frequent diagnosis or add ratings for every label. If two candidates remain plausible, show the precise criterion distinguishing them and ask the examiner.

4.4 Convert clinical findings to modifiers

For the original Sixth Edition method, the potential modifiers are functional history (GMFH), physical examination (GMPE), and clinical studies (GMCS). Each comes from its own criteria. The number describes a category; it is neither a percentage nor a confidence score.

Physician-facing controlSystem behavior
Function: proposed category + evidenceUse history and the applicable instrument to propose GMFH. Apply reliability/consistency rules before using it. A PDQ score cannot simply become a percentage or generic 0–4 modifier.
Exam: confirmed current findingsMap the measured findings to GMPE using the selected chapter. Show which findings determined the category. Reuse the exam entry rather than asking for a number.
Studies: findings and relationship to diagnosisMap qualifying study findings to GMCS. Apply the specific exclusion rules. Evidence supporting the diagnosis does not automatically mean the entire studies modifier is excluded.
Missing / excludedStore these separately. Excluded means the rules say to omit that term, with a reason. Missing means it has not been established; ask for the fact or apply an explicitly permitted method. Neither means zero.

Calculate the sum of eligible (modifier − class) terms. Move that many grades from C, subject to the selected method's limits and exceptions, then retrieve the cell's percentage. Expose this arithmetic under the clinical answers so the physician can inspect it. [3]

4.6 Rating rules and stored output

Add a small versioned reference module per supported region: canonical-diagnosis-to-row mapping; entry and class criteria; modifier criteria and exclusions; grade-to-value lookup; units; combination/conversion rules; exact table/footnote references. Validate the licensed content against known worked cases. This is the part queries and AI cannot supply on demand.

Save methodVersion, region, diagnosisId, rowId, class, criteriaAnswers, modifiers[{value, status, reason, evidenceRefs}], adjustment, grade, impairmentValue, impairmentUnit, approval. For multiple regions, preserve component worksheets and apply permitted combination rules rather than adding percentages. A condition not at MMI remains deferred; a permitted partial rating must identify its limited scope.

5. Report drafting and review

Build an explicit question-to-decision map. For each referral question, store the original wording, subparts, linked decision IDs, and answer status. The 15 canonical questions provide the default topic map; bespoke questions add requirements instead of disappearing into the standard list.

Report sectionInputsGeneration
History and record reviewExisting case summary, treatment episodes, studies and source records.Condense the relevant chronology with record references. Distinguish the claimant's account from documented history.
Current examinationVisit note, confirmed findings, function and questionnaires.Generate the subjective/objective/functional narrative from today's inputs.
OpinionsApproved diagnosis, causation, MMI, prognosis, care and work decisions.Reuse exact decisions; AI writes their explanation from the approved rationale and cited facts.
ImpairmentApproved worksheet or reason deferred.Insert method, criteria, calculation and result deterministically; narrative explains their application.
Referral answersOriginal questions + linked decisions.For each question: direct answer, basis, limitation if any. Preserve distinctions such as “contributed” versus “primary cause.”

Review screen: report on the left; referral coverage on the right. Clicking an answer opens its decision and evidence. Highlight unanswered subparts and conflicts. A missing opinion can become an explicitly approved “cannot determine” answer with a reason. Check dates, percentages, units and reference IDs against the saved packet before displaying the draft.

Editing: a changed clinical conclusion must update its decision before regenerating linked sections. Preserve physician prose edits for reconciliation. Changing an exam fact or rating input marks dependent approvals stale. Final sign-off freezes the report and its inputs.
