### 17-21-03 Lumbar Radiculopathy Involving the L1 Nerve Root

**17-21-03 class 1A** (2% WPI) **CH** (residual symptoms) with mechanism of injury consistent with DX;

- **and PE** confirms residual radicular symptoms of pain or subjectively altered sensory perception (paresthesia-like often described as numbness) without objectively verifiable sensory loss for dullness to pinprick or loss of sharp vs dull perception in a neuroanatomically plausible dermatomal distribution compatible with the L1 dermatome (ie, lower back or upper lumbar region into the medial groin, front of upper thigh, minimal motor weakness, but individuals may report difficulty with hip flexion);
- **and CS** (one of the following)
  - MRI or CT imaging demonstrating an L1 nerve root injury or lesion
  - electrodiagnostic findings confirming L1 nerve root pathology consistent with Guides CS definitions per Section 17.06e1 Electrodiagnosis of Radiculopathy. Although this test can assist in supporting the diagnosis, it is rarely performed and often fails to provide definitive confirmation. The primary reason lies in the technical challenges involved, as the nerve root primarily innervates the iliopsoas muscle, unlike lower lumbar nerve roots with representation in limb muscles
  - surgeon's clear objective verification of an intraoperative lesion affecting the L1 nerve root

### 17-21-10 Lumbar Stenosis With Neurogenic Claudication

Lumbar central canal stenosis may present with a history of **neurogenic claudication**. There are often no reliable objective findings on physical examination, and the diagnosis is based on imaging findings that explain the reported symptoms.177

In order to rate symptomatic central canal spinal stenosis, there must be a discrete event that precipitated symptoms. At time of current evaluation, the evaluator determines the CH, PE, and relevant CS to be consistent with the diagnosis of spinal stenosis and the natural history of the condition; +/− surgery.

**17-21-10 class 1A** (2% WPI) **CH**: A discrete event and plausible mechanism of injury, after which there are complaints of neurogenic claudication (pain in the buttocks and posterior thighs that increases with walking or extension and is relieved by sitting or bending forward, and may include reports of fatigue, heaviness, weakness and/or paresthesias). There are no reliable objective physical examination or imaging findings for this diagnosis, and the evaluator determines that the individual presents consistently and is believable. There should be documentation on more than one occasion of symptoms increased with walking and improved with flexion/sitting.

- **and CS:** imaging or findings at surgery consistent with DX of spinal stenosis at single level consistent with clinical history listed above

**17-21-10 class 1B** (4% WPI) **CH**: A discrete event and plausible mechanism of injury, after which there are complaints of neurogenic claudication (pain in the buttocks and posterior thighs that increases with walking or extension and is relieved by sitting or bending forward, and may include reports of fatigue, heaviness, weakness and/or paresthesias). There are no reliable objective physical examination or imaging findings for this diagnosis, and the evaluator determines that the individual presents consistently and is believable. There should be documentation on more than one occasion of symptoms increased with walking and improved with flexion/sitting.

- **and CS:** imaging or findings at surgery consistent with DX of spinal stenosis at multiple levels or bilaterally consistent with clinical history listed above.

### 17-21-11 Lumbar AOMSI Associated With Fusion, Arthroplasty, or Pseudarthrosis

The calculated impairment values take into account the potential future stress on the disc spaces adjacent to the surgical site, a condition known as adjacent disc disease or adjacent segment degeneration. This consideration reflects the understanding that the redistribution of mechanical forces following a spinal procedure can accelerate wear and tear on neighboring vertebral levels, potentially leading to additional spinal issues in the future.
