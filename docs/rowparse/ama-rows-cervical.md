### 17-19-03 Cervical Radiculopathy Involving the C1 Nerve Root (Very Rare)

Due to the unique anatomical position and function of the C1 nerve root, radiculopathy is extremely rare. When affected, it often overlaps with upper cervical or occipital conditions.

**17-19-03 class 1A** (2% WPI) **CH** (residual symptoms) with mechanism of injury consistent with DX;

- **and PE** confirms residual radicular symptoms of pain or subjectively altered sensory perception (paresthesia-like often described as numbness) without objectively verifiable sensory loss for dullness to pinprick or loss of sharp vs dull perception in a neuroanatomically plausible dermatomal distribution compatible with the C1 dermatome (ie, occipital or upper neck region, radiating from the back of the head toward the crown or forehead, with no significant limb symptoms);
- **and** **CS** (one of the following)
  - MRI or CT imaging demonstrating a C1 nerve root injury or lesion
  - electrodiagnostic findings confirming C1 nerve root pathology consistent with *Guides* CS definitions per Section 17.06e1 Electrodiagnosis of Radiculopathy. Although this test can assist in supporting the diagnosis, it is rarely performed and often fails to provide definitive confirmation. The primary reason lies in the technical challenges involved, as the nerve root primarily innervates muscles in the neck region and, unlike lower cervical nerve roots, lacks representation in limb muscles.
  - surgeon's clear objective verification of an intraoperative lesion involving the C1 nerve root

### 17-19-11 Cervical Spinal Fracture C1 or C2

Fractures may occur at single or multiple levels. The single fracture with the highest whole person impairment rating is used to calculate impairment.

**17-19-11 class 1A** (2% WPI) **CH** (residual symptoms) with mechanism of injury consistent with DX;

- **and PE** consistent with DX;
- **and CS** (one of the following) imaging **or** findings at surgery consistent with DX of
  - healed odontoid process fracture
  - nondisplaced lamina, pedicle or articular process (posterior elements) fracture without loss of structural integrity
  - displaced spinous process (SP) or transverse process (TP) (posterior elements) fracture without loss of structural integrity

**17-19-11 class 1B** (4% WPI) **CH** (residual symptoms) with mechanism of injury consistent with DX;

- **and PE** consistent with DX;
- **and CS** imaging or findings at surgery consistent with DX of posterior element fracture (lamina, pedicle, articular process, spinous process, or transverse process) without dislocation that has healed with or without surgery, often result in range of motion loss. A common complaint after these injuries is difficulty driving due to limited right and left cervical rotation.

**17-19-11 class 2A** (10% WPI) **CH** (residual symptoms) with mechanism of injury consistent with DX;

- **and PE** consistent with DX;
- **and CS** imaging or findings at surgery consistent with DX of a posterior element fracture, posterior arch of C1 and pars or pedicle of C2 with displacement disrupting the spinal canal and fracture has healed with or without surgery.

Unlike those of C3 through C7, common fracture patterns for C1 and C2 do not involve loss of vertebral body height. There is rarely neurologic deficit, as there are no motor branches of significance from C1 and C2 roots. The sensory branches/roots are usually uninjured, or there is so much overlap of sensory innervation the deficit is undetectable. There is no confirmatory electrodiagnostic test for the C1 or C2 roots.
