### 17-20-03 Thoracic Radiculopathy Involving the T1 Nerve Root

At time of the current evaluation, the evaluator determines the CH, PE, and relevant CS to be consistent with the diagnosis of thoracic radiculopathy involving a T1 nerve root injury or lesion and the natural history of the condition. Ratings for T1 nerve root pathology are similar to those for the cervical spine, because upper limb function is affected. Ratings for T2-T12 are rated separately.

**17-20-03 class 1A** (2% WPI) **CH** (residual symptoms) with mechanism of injury consistent with DX;

- **and PE** confirms residual radicular symptoms of pain or subjectively altered sensory perception (paresthesia-like often described as numbness) without objectively verifiable sensory loss for dullness to pinprick or loss of sharp vs dull perception in a neuroanatomically plausible dermatomal distribution compatible with the T1 dermatome (ie, upper half of medial forearm, medial portion of arm);
- **and CS** (one of the following)
  - MRI or CT imaging demonstrating a T1 nerve root injury or lesion
  - electrodiagnostic findings confirming T1 nerve root pathology consistent with Guides CS definitions per Section 17.06e1 Electrodiagnosis of Radiculopathy. If testing meets criteria for a T1 radiculopathy by AANEM criteria247,248, this is objective confirmation of radiculopathy. If the date of injury and date of testing align, this should be rated as radiculopathy.
  - surgeon's clear objective verification of an intraoperative lesion demonstrating the T1 nerve root

**17-20-03 class 1B** (4% WPI) **CH** (residual symptoms) with mechanism of injury consistent with DX;

- **and PE** sensory deficit dullness to pinprick sensation in the dermatomal distribution compatible with the T1 dermatome (ie, upper half of medial forearm, medial portion of arm) while sharp vs dull perception remains intact;
- **and CS** (one of the following)

### 17-20-04 Thoracic Radiculopathy Involving the T2-T12 Nerve Roots

**17-20-04 class 1A** (2% WPI) **CH** (residual symptoms) with mechanism of injury consistent with DX;

- **and PE** confirms residual radicular symptoms of pain or subjectively altered sensory perception (paresthesia-like often described as numbness) without objectively verifiable sensory loss for dullness to pinprick or loss of sharp vs dull perception in a neuroanatomically plausible dermatomal distribution compatible with the T2-T12 dermatome (ie, unilateral dermatomal pain compatible with the level of the T2-T12 nerve root involved, radiating around the torso toward the anterior midline);
- **and CS** (one of the following)
  - MRI or CT imaging demonstrating a T2-T12 nerve root injury or lesion
  - electrodiagnostic findings confirming T2-T12 nerve root pathology consistent with Guides CS definitions per Section 17.06e1 Electrodiagnosis of Radiculopathy. Although this test can assist in supporting the diagnosis, it is rarely performed and often fails to provide definitive confirmation. The primary reason lies in the technical challenges as the involved nerve roots lacks representation in limb muscles.
  - surgeon's clear objective verification of an intraoperative lesion demonstrating the T2-T12 nerve root

- **and PE** unilateral dermatomal sensory deficit dullness to pinprick sensation in the dermatomal distribution compatible with the level of the T2-T12 dermatome (ie, unilateral dermatomal pain compatible with the level of the T2-T12 nerve root involved, radiating around the torso toward the anterior midline) while sharp vs dull perception remains intact;
- **and CS** (one of the following)
  - MRI or CT imaging demonstrating a T2-T12 nerve root injury or lesion

Myelopathy secondary to a lesion in the thoracic spine is rated in the Chapter 13 The Nervous System. In the event that radiculopathy or myelopathy in the thoracic spine is the result of a fracture, the impairment rating for myelopathy is combined with the impairment rating for the appropriate fracture.

### 17-20-05 Thoracic Spinal Fracture

Fractures may occur at single or multiple levels. The single fracture with the highest whole person impairment rating is used to calculate impairment.

**17-20-05 class 1A** (2% WPI) **CH** (residual symptoms) with mechanism of injury consistent with DX;

- **and PE** consistent with DX;
- **and CS** (one of the following) imaging or findings at surgery consistent with DX
  - <25% compression of any single vertebral body or multiple vertebral bodies, with or without bony retropulsion into the canal, healed with or without surgery (includes vertebroplasty or kyphoplasty)
  - nondisplaced lamina, pedicle or articular process (posterior elements) fracture without loss of structural integrity
  - displaced spinous process (SP) or transverse process (TP) (posterior elements) fracture without loss of structural integrity

**17-20-05 class 1B** (4% WPI) **CH** (residual symptoms) with mechanism of injury consistent with DX;

- **and PE** consistent with DX;
- **and CS** (one of the following) imaging or findings at surgery consistent with DX;
  - ≥25% to <50% compression single vertebral body or multiple vertebral bodies, with or without bony retropulsion into the canal, healed with or without surgery (includes vertebroplasty or kyphoplasty)
  - displaced lamina, pedicle or articular process (posterior elements) fracture without loss of structural integrity

**17-20-05 class 2A** (10% WPI) **CH** (residual symptoms) with mechanism of injury consistent with DX;

- **and PE** consistent with DX;
- **and CS** imaging or findings at surgery consistent with DX of ≥50% compression of any single vertebral body or multiple vertebral bodies with or without bony retropulsion into the canal, pedicle and or posterior element fracture, healed with or without surgery (includes vertebroplasty or kyphoplasty)

### 17-20-06 Thoracic AOMSI Associated With Fusion, Arthroplasty, or Pseudarthrosis

The calculated impairment values take into account the potential future stress on the disc spaces adjacent to the surgical site, a condition known as adjacent disc disease or adjacent segment degeneration. This consideration reflects the understanding that the redistribution of mechanical forces following a spinal procedure can accelerate wear and tear on neighboring vertebral levels, potentially leading to additional spinal issues in the future.

**17-20-06 class 1A** (3% WPI) **CH** (residual symptoms) with mechanism of injury and physical examination consistent with DX;

- **and PE** consistent with DX;
- **and CS** imaging or findings at surgery consistent with DX of single-level thoracic fusion or arthroplasty

**17-20-06 class 1B** (6% WPI) **CH** (residual symptoms) with mechanism of injury and physical examination consistent with DX;

- **and PE** consistent with DX;
- **and CS** imaging or findings at surgery consistent with DX of multi-level thoracic fusion or arthroplasty

### 17-20-07 Thoracic AOMSI Associated With Instability

The calculated impairment values take into account the potential future stress on the disc spaces adjacent to the surgical site, a condition known as adjacent disc disease or adjacent segment degeneration. This consideration reflects the understanding that the redistribution of mechanical forces following a spinal procedure can accelerate wear and tear on neighboring vertebral levels, potentially leading to additional spinal issues in the future.

**17-20-07 class 1A** (2% WPI) **CH** (residual symptoms) with mechanism of injury and physical examination consistent with DX;

- **and PE** consistent with DX;
- **and CS** by flexion and extension radiographs (one of the following)
  - Relative-translation AOMSI-translation measurements requires a greater than 2.5 mm anterior or 2.5 mm posterior relative translation of one vertebra on another
  - Angular-motion AOMSI-angular motion of more than 5° greater than either adjacent level
