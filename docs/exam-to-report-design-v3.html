AZIME · DESIGN REVISION 3 · 10 OCTOBER 2026

Exam-to-report implementation design

Keep regional analysis tracks. Use the shared visit's Subjective, Objective and Functional sections as each region's latest trajectory checkpoint. Analyze MMI by diagnosis; let the physician issue an opinion for one diagnosis or assess selected diagnoses together.

New version of revision 2. Incorporates PDQ provenance, diagnosis changes, regional processing, and grouped MMI opinions. The AMA walkthrough retains the original Sixth Edition 2008/2009 method.

An “all conditions at MMI” value is a derived report summary, not a processing gate. Preserve mixed statuses. Case-level synthesis reconciles shared care and work restrictions and answers cross-region referral questions; AMA rules determine whether and how rating components combine.

2. Shared examination and the latest trajectory checkpoint

Build a shared note with Subjective, Objective and Functional sections, organized by region. Dictation can populate these sections. Keep common history in one shared block; do not apply an unlabeled regional finding to every region.

SectionToday's checkpointSource distinction
SubjectivePain, numbness, symptom frequency, perceived improvement or worsening.Patient-reported symptoms.
ObjectiveExamination findings: strength, reflexes, sensation, gait, relevant measurements and tests.Observed/measured examination evidence; distinguish historical findings from today's findings.
FunctionalWalking, standing, sitting, lifting, work and daily activities; relevant questionnaire responses.Record patient-reported versus observed/measured. “Reports standing for 15 minutes” differs from observed performance.

PDQ is subjective evidence about function. Attach it to the functional checkpoint with its date and instrument version. It supplements the history; agreement between two self-reports is not independent objective confirmation. Keep a case-wide PDQ case-wide unless the patient or examiner supplies regional attribution. Do not split its total across diagnoses or regions.

Trajectory update: supply the prior regional trajectory, its evidence, and today's three sections to the post-exam pass. Return updated direction, evidence quality and interpretation for each domain. Account for measurement differences; do not treat missing values as normal or assume a numeric trend from unlike instruments.

Additional structured inputs: beside the note, show only unanswered VisitPriority items, referral gaps and likely rating criteria. Extract proposed answers from the note for confirmation. Precise muscle/side/grade, level or activity limits are required when a rating or restriction needs them; they are not prerequisites for a narrative trajectory update.

Save action: save the shared visit and regional checkpoints, then review diagnoses before finalizing the post-exam input snapshot.

3. Diagnosis review and targeted reanalysis

Show each in-scope diagnosis with Confirm / Reject / Uncertain, plus Add diagnosis. Capture region, canonical mapping, side/level where relevant, and a short reason for a change. Keep diagnoses documented in the records separate from the examiner's current diagnosis set. A resolved condition is not automatically a historically incorrect diagnosis.

ActionAnalysis response
ConfirmKeep existing diagnosis-dependent work unless other inputs changed.
Add / replaceRerun the affected region's care-taxonomy pass with the revised active diagnosis set, existing extractions, and the physician's exam/reasoning. Rebuild the regional treatment union and diagnosis assessments; supersede replaced diagnoses.
Reject onlyRetire its current diagnosis assessments and identify dependent conclusions. Refresh the regional ladder and affected analysis with the remaining diagnoses. Do not erase documented past diagnoses or treatments.
Uncertain / no taxonomy matchRetain the unresolved diagnosis and its effect on decisions. Use a physician-entered care assessment where needed; do not silently omit the diagnosis from analysis.

Rerun sequence: diagnosis revision → affected regional care analysis → physician review of changed treatment assessments → remaining-care query → post-exam analysis → MMI review → affected rating/report sections. A full rerun of the affected regional pass is simpler than a new per-diagnosis orchestration system. Leave unrelated regions alone unless the change also alters their inputs.

Preserve: extracted records, treatment episodes, visit, questionnaire responses and historical trajectory checkpoints. Carry prior treatment reviews as context, but do not silently retain approval where the diagnostic basis changed. Recompute interpretations as needed. Past treatment appropriateness under the diagnosis made at the time is a separate question from care needed now.

After the visit: do not require a new exam-preparation pass. Assemble a fresh post-exam snapshot from corrected scope and reviewed care, retaining earlier prep as history. Newly exposed gaps become post-exam questions. The report explains the examiner's disagreement or diagnostic evolution; it does not automatically declare prior misdiagnosis.

4. Post-exam output and physician MMI decisions

4.1 Build one regional result containing diagnosis-level proposals

Each regional result contains updated subjective/objective/functional trajectories and a list of {diagnosisId, plateauEvidence, mmiPosture, unresolvedQuestions}. Label plateau evidence as diagnosis-specific, shared regional evidence, or attribution uncertain. Regional stability must not become fabricated proof that every diagnosis has plateaued.

Candidate remaining care comes from a query. For active diagnoses and the latest reviewed treatment assessments, select applicable treatments with not_tried or inadequately_trialed adequacy. Use physician-resolved values rather than raw AI suggestions. Put unclear applicability/adequacy into an unresolved bucket; keep contraindicated/not-applicable items out of the candidate list. Include physician-added treatments.

Return treatment-row ID, affected diagnosis IDs, basis and existing rationale. Deduplicate shared treatment rows while retaining per-diagnosis assessments. Feed these candidates into the post-exam pass and display them alongside each diagnosis. The list is not itself a prescription or a reason to deny MMI; ongoing or supportive care may also require separate review.

4.2 Let the physician choose the scope before deciding MMI

Show region → diagnosis → proposed MMI → plateau evidence → candidate remaining care. The physician selects diagnoses and chooses “Assess separately” or “Assess together.” Together creates one opinion covering those diagnosis IDs in that region. It does not merge diagnoses, trajectories, causation opinions or AMA rating rows.

MMI decisionRequired details for its selected scope
At MMIEffective date, rationale and prognosis. Classify outstanding care as supportive, not recommended, recovery-directed, or uncertain; explain anything that appears inconsistent with the MMI opinion.
Not at MMIRecovery-directed plan, expected benefit, duration and reassessment interval/event. Defer permanent rating for that scope.
Cannot determineMissing fact, affected decision, resolution action and review trigger.

Grouping is useful when diagnoses describe overlapping regional symptoms or the examiner can support one conclusion for them. It does not turn recovery-directed care into maintenance. Keep the original AI proposals and the physician's explanation for resolving any disagreement.

Each diagnosis has its own date/rationale or care/reassessment fields. Switching scope preserves these selections as a draft; only the selected scope can be approved.

Draft report summary

Thoracic track: unchanged. Its diagnoses, MMI opinions and recommendations remain independent.

Approval rule: every active diagnosis must be covered by exactly one current approved MMI opinion, or remain visibly pending. A group can cover a subset of a region. Changing membership invalidates that opinion's approval and dependent rating/report sections. A “whole lumbar region” statement is available only when all active lumbar diagnoses are included or otherwise explicitly accounted for.

MMI is not rating eligibility. If disc herniation is at MMI while strain remains active, the rating review must establish whether the findings used for the disc rating are stable and separable and whether the method/reporting rules permit a limited rating. Otherwise defer the regional rating. Do not imply that a per-diagnosis MMI opinion automatically produces an independent impairment percentage. [4]

6. AMA impairment: what to build

Build a regional worksheet that asks clinical questions and derives the rating coordinates. The default view should show the selected diagnosis, the facts responsible for its classification, any unanswered criterion, and the resulting percentage. Put numeric modifier controls in an expanded “Rating detail” view.

6.1 Explain the four rating terms in the UI

TermMeaningExample of system behavior
Diagnosis rowThe condition being rated within the regional table.Canonical “disc herniation” retrieves candidate rows. Check each row's entry criteria and footnotes before selecting it.
ClassThe severity category within that diagnosis row. It sets the available impairment range.Use confirmed clinical criteria to select the class. Do not ask the physician to choose an unexplained number.
GradeThe position A–E within the selected class. The original method generally starts at C and adjusts using eligible modifiers.Calculate the movement after functional history, examination and clinical studies are assessed.
Impairment percentageThe value at the selected row + class + grade, in the unit specified by the table.Read the percentage from that cell. A one-grade change is not necessarily one percentage point.

Existing questionnaire change: definitions.ts uses abbreviated PDQ prompts and an answered-item denominator. Verify the administered instrument, item wording, anchors, completeness and scoring before mapping it to AMA criteria. Until then, retain it as patient-reported function. The app's regional questionnaires should not be treated as validated named instruments without verification.

6.3 Clinical questions determine the row and class

For a lumbar disc candidate, the worksheet needs the history of objective radiculopathy, its current status, clinical correlation, levels/sides involved, and relevant surgery. Present these as labeled choices with source-backed selections. Then apply the full table criteria. A single question such as “radiculopathy present?” is too coarse.

Why this matters: MRI herniation alone may follow the nonspecific-pain pathway. Prior objective radiculopathy, now resolved, can qualify for the disc-herniation Class 1 pathway. Retrieve the historical findings. [2]

Row selection: preserve all accepted clinical diagnoses, but select the ratable diagnosis according to the regional rules. Do not automatically select the most frequent diagnosis or add ratings for every label. If two candidates remain plausible, show the precise criterion distinguishing them and ask the examiner.

6.4 Convert clinical findings to modifiers

For the original Sixth Edition method, the potential modifiers are functional history (GMFH), physical examination (GMPE), and clinical studies (GMCS). Each comes from its own criteria. The number describes a category; it is neither a percentage nor a confidence score.

Physician-facing controlSystem behavior
Function: proposed category + evidenceUse history and the applicable instrument to propose GMFH. Apply reliability/consistency rules before using it. A PDQ score cannot simply become a percentage or generic 0–4 modifier.
Exam: confirmed current findingsMap the measured findings to GMPE using the selected chapter. Show which findings determined the category. Reuse the exam entry rather than asking for a number.
Studies: findings and relationship to diagnosisMap qualifying study findings to GMCS. Apply the specific exclusion rules. Evidence supporting the diagnosis does not automatically mean the entire studies modifier is excluded.
Missing / excludedStore these separately. Excluded means the rules say to omit that term, with a reason. Missing means it has not been established; ask for the fact or apply an explicitly permitted method. Neither means zero.

Calculate the sum of eligible (modifier − class) terms. Move that many grades from C, subject to the selected method's limits and exceptions, then retrieve the cell's percentage. Expose this arithmetic under the clinical answers so the physician can inspect it. [3]

6.6 Rating rules and stored output

Add a small versioned reference module per supported region: canonical-diagnosis-to-row mapping; entry and class criteria; modifier criteria and exclusions; grade-to-value lookup; units; combination/conversion rules; exact table/footnote references. Validate the licensed content against known worked cases. This is the part queries and AI cannot supply on demand.

Save methodVersion, region, diagnosisId, mmiOpinionIds, eligibilityStatus, eligibilityRationale, rowId, class, criteriaAnswers, modifiers[{value, status, reason, evidenceRefs}], adjustment, grade, impairmentValue, impairmentUnit, approval. Retain independent regional worksheets. Combine eligible components only as prescribed by the selected AMA method, with their units and overlap rules. If a region remains unrateable, identify any permitted limited result as partial; do not label it the final combined case rating. Grouping diagnoses for an MMI opinion never determines which AMA row to rate.

7. Report drafting and review

Build an explicit question-to-decision map. For each referral question, store the original wording, subparts, linked decision IDs, and answer status. The 15 canonical questions provide the default topic map; bespoke questions add requirements instead of disappearing into the standard list.

Report sectionInputsGeneration
History and record reviewExisting case summary, treatment episodes, studies and source records.Condense the relevant chronology with record references. Distinguish the claimant's account from documented history.
Current examinationVisit note, confirmed findings, function and questionnaires.Generate the subjective/objective/functional narrative from today's inputs.
OpinionsApproved diagnoses, scoped MMI opinions, causation, prognosis, care and work decisions.Preserve diagnosis membership and mixed regional statuses; AI writes explanations from approved rationale and facts. Describe disagreement with recorded diagnoses without rewriting history.
ImpairmentRegional eligibility decisions, approved worksheets, combination result or reason deferred.Insert method, criteria, calculation and result deterministically; narrative explains their application.
Referral answersOriginal questions + linked decisions.For each question: direct answer, basis, limitation if any. Preserve distinctions such as “contributed” versus “primary cause.”

Case synthesis: deduplicate shared recommendations while preserving their diagnosis links; reconcile conflicting restrictions into one physician-approved work-capacity opinion. A shared functional observation or PDQ response must retain its source and original scope.

Review screen: report on the left; referral coverage on the right. Clicking an answer opens its decision and evidence. Highlight unanswered subparts and conflicts. A missing opinion can become an explicitly approved “cannot determine” answer with a reason. Check dates, percentages, units and reference IDs against the saved packet before displaying the draft.

Editing: a changed clinical conclusion must update its decision before regenerating linked sections. Preserve physician prose edits for reconciliation. Changing an exam fact or rating input marks dependent approvals stale. Final sign-off freezes the report and its inputs.

Build sequence

  1. Regional visit checkpoints and diagnosis review. Support S/O/F sections, evidence provenance and diagnosis additions/rejections. Existing visit contracts already have confirm/reject reviews; extend them rather than adding a second review system.
  2. Diagnosis-change orchestration. Refresh affected regional care analysis, retain historical artifacts and surface changed assessments for review. Replace the current requirement to regenerate exam prep after rejection with a post-visit reconciliation path and a fresh snapshot.
  3. Post-exam analysis and MMI scope. Generate regional trajectories and diagnosis-level proposals; attach deterministic care candidates; add separate/together MMI decisions. Keep runtime instructions in prompt modules and validate output against the updated schema.
  4. Rating eligibility, calculator and report. Reuse the Sixth Edition worksheet flow below the scoped opinions. Draft from approved decisions, with no re-inference of MMI from prose.

Checks before release: mixed lumbar/thoracic status; two lumbar diagnoses assessed separately and together; grouping changed after approval; diagnosis replacement after the visit; shared care row deduplication; uncertain care excluded from deterministic conclusions; case-wide PDQ not duplicated as regional evidence; at-MMI diagnosis with an unrateable regional worksheet. Changed dependencies must mark affected approvals and draft sections stale.

These are proposed changes. The current post-exam contract still has one plateau/MMI pair per regional result and obtains its context through the refined-analysis snapshot; both the output shape and that input dependency need updating.
